Why it exists

AI makes first drafts cheap. The harder work is understanding the problem, protecting intent, handling real users and states, proving accessibility, and knowing what is actually ready to ship.

The standard is designed to make that reasoning inspectable by people and machines.

Human + machine

Autonomy without opacity.

AI-assisted products need clear boundaries around suggestion, generation, inspection, verification and execution.

Suggested → Generated → Inspected → Verified → Executed → Human-approved

These states keep important actions and decisions legible. The goal is not less automation; it is better-bounded automation with visible scope, evidence, uncertainty, provenance and human control where risk requires it.

How it works

From vague idea to evidence-backed product.

The workflow works at different levels of depth: plain language for beginners, structured intent for designers and developers, and machine-readable contracts for agents.

01 — Understand

Start with the real problem

Clarify users, jobs, goals, constraints and success criteria.

02 — Research

Use evidence, not guesses

Check users, existing systems, platform conventions, accessibility guidance and relevant alternatives.

03 — Specify

Make intent explicit

Capture what is required, what is locked and what remains uncertain.

04 — Design

Create deliberate directions

Use the standard, contracts and patterns to make purposeful choices.

05 — Compare & validate

Challenge the result

Check product fit, UX, visual quality, accessibility, responsive behaviour, content and engineering reality.

06 — Build, test & learn

Ship what survives

Test the implementation, record evidence and feed useful outcomes back into the system.

Inclusive by construction

Design one product for many ways of using it.

Accessibility is part of structure and behaviour, not a final decoration pass.

Input

Keyboard, touch, pointer, assistive technology and other applicable input modes are part of interaction design.

Perception

Semantics, contrast, zoom, reflow, understandable status and non-colour-dependent meaning are considered from the start.

Cognition

Clear language, predictable flows, manageable complexity, useful adaptation and recovery support diverse users.

Resilience

Long content, missing media, empty/loading/error states, localization and degraded conditions are treated as normal.

A living standard

v0.1 is a starting point, not a final answer.

Changes should be justified by research, benchmarks, production evidence or community feedback. The standard stays versioned so improvements are traceable rather than silently changing underneath users and agents.

Questions

Useful whether you design, build or direct.

Who is this for?

People with ideas, professional designers, developers and AI agents that need a shared language for design intent and quality.

Is this another AI website builder?

No. The Studio is one product surface, but the wider system focuses on research, intent, evaluation, accessibility, production quality and learning.

Can agents use it?

Yes. The public standard includes machine-readable discovery and durable contracts designed so agents can retrieve explicit intent instead of inferring a design system from screenshots.

Does it guarantee great design?

No. It makes quality decisions more explicit and testable. Outcomes still depend on product understanding, evidence, implementation and real validation.
